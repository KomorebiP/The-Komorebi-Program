## Exact integral generators and reconstruction

Compute the upper triangular row Hermite basis H of the row lattice of A=D T_active, with positive pivots and reduced entries above pivots. Incremental row insertion uses exact Bezout row operations of determinant one. Track the integer coefficients U throughout. Solving triangular systems gives an integer C such that

    H = U A,   A = C H,   U C = I_55.

The first two identities prove equality of row lattices in both directions, independently of the particular HNF algorithm. The third follows from full row rank and is also directly checked. H/D is therefore a Z-basis of the full active row lattice. With a_old the old independent-coordinate vector, the new active coordinate vector is

    z = (H/D) a_old = U a_active.

Consequently a_active = C z, and z is an integer combination of the original counting sequences. Adjoin the 217 unchanged coordinates to obtain integer matrices C_full (537 x 272) and U_full (272 x 537), with U_full C_full=I_272. This is a rank-minimal basis of the integral span of the original series. The new generators may have negative coefficients and need not themselves be counts of individual combinatorial objects. Nor do they necessarily form a subset of the original catalogue.

The matrix W is defined by W H = D I_55. It is integral because the original basis occurs among the rows. Hence a_old = W z. The quotient L / Z^272 is finite, with invariant factors given by the Smith normal form of W (transpose conventions do not affect invariant factors). The canonical HNF basis and the resulting C,U,W are all retained in INTEGRAL_TRANSFER_LATTICE.json.

The lattice index [L:Z^272] is 3841431346192362519895474176000000. The nonunit invariant factors are 6, 6, 12, 720, 2880, 5760, 2488320, 299195895398400. ## Independent Smith certificate

For p dividing D, write e=v_p(D). Reduce W modulo p^e, eliminate using the least valuation pivot and multiply by a unit to normalize it to p^v. Row and column operations are invertible over Z/p^e Z. A residual zero block has valuation e because the inclusion has exponent dividing D.

The verifier also works directly on the original rectangular scaled matrix A. It eliminates unit pivots modulo p^e, divides the residual block by p, and repeats. The resulting multiplicities give the local Smith valuations of the scaled row lattice. Complementing those valuations in e and reversing their order gives the inclusion valuations of W. This provides an independent check from the input matrix rather than relying only on the HNF output. For primes not dividing D, the inclusion is invertible locally.

## Sharp precision theorem

Work modulo powers of a prime p. Consider any compatible old-basis residue data b modulo p^(h+k), with h>=1, k>=0. Compatibility means that there exists an integral new coordinate z satisfying W_full z=b modulo p^(h+k). By local Smith reduction, change coordinates by matrices invertible over the p-adic integers. The difference between two solutions must satisfy, independently in each transformed coordinate,

    p^e_i delta_z_i = 0 mod p^(h+k).

Thus delta_z_i is divisible by p^max(0,h+k-e_i). Its image modulo p^h has exactly p^max(0,min(h,e_i-k)) possible values. The choices multiply. The full catalogue coordinate vector is C_full z modulo p^h, with U_full as a left inverse, so distinct new-coordinate residues give distinct complete catalogue vectors. The number of possible complete vectors is exactly

    p^(sum_i max(0,min(h,e_i-k))).

This is a theorem about all compatible lattice data; the actual sequence evaluations occupy a possibly smaller set. It is not a statement about the number of indices sharing a residue pattern. Full recovery is guaranteed when k>=max e_i; if k is smaller, some compatible inputs have more than one output. For each of the six bad primes, the checker constructs a primitive active vector v with W v divisible by p^e but not p^(e+1), where e is the largest valuation, and verifies an actual catalogue coordinate of C v nonzero modulo p. Multiplying v by p^(h-1) demonstrates sharp loss of e digits for any target precision h. The witness is a lattice-coordinate difference, not a claim that two actual counting-sequence indices realize that difference.

Using the new integral coordinates, all forward and reverse transfers use integer operations, so reconstruction commutes with reduction modulo every positive integer. There is no required additional precision. This resolves the lattice and precision-loss problem for the retained transfer matrix.

The initial local Smith implementation had an assignment-to-constant error when normalizing a pivot; the corrected implementation passed the complete replay. No arithmetic result from the failed run was retained.

Remaining boundaries: the HNF generator lift has weights as large as 242 decimal digits and reconstruction weights as large as 18 digits. This establishes exact denominator-free reconstruction, not shortest, sparse, fast, or nonnegative generator formulas. A basis selected exclusively from the original 537 records, optimal integral-coordinate choice, universal rank in arbitrary parameters, and Lean formalization remain separate questions.
