## 1. Rank reduction and saturation

Let $W$ be an integral $m\times N$ matrix of rational rank $r>0$. Write $L$ for the lattice generated by its rows and $S=(L\otimes\mathbb Q)\cap\mathbb Z^N$ for its saturation. Choose an integral row basis $L_0$ of $L$ and an integral row basis $S_0$ of $S$. Put $H=L_0L_0^\top$, $G=S_0S_0^\top$. There is a nonsingular integral matrix $P$ of size $r$ with $L_0=PS_0$; explicitly $P=L_0S_0^\top G^{-1}$. The Smith factors of $W$ that are nonzero are those of $P$, and those of $WW^\top$ that are nonzero are those of $H$. Indeed the primitive basis $S_0$ extends to an integral basis of the ambient lattice, and unimodular row operations reduce $W$ to $(L_0;0)$. The rank-zero case has no nonzero factors. Thus

$$H=PGP^\top,\qquad \det H=[S:L]^2\det G.$$

The group $S/L$ records source divisibility. The positive definite $G$ records the geometry of the saturated rational span, which need not be unimodular. When $r=N$, $G$ is unimodular, and the earlier square theory is recovered. When $r<N$, redundant row coefficients cannot be uniquely recovered even from exact data; precision statements below concern a chosen independent row basis.

For a symmetric integral adjacency operator $A$ and a root unit vector $e_o$, use rows $e_o^\top A^j$, $0\le j<N$. The first $r$ rows are a basis of the rational cyclic space. Its minimal annihilator $M(x)$ is a monic divisor over $\mathbb Q[x]$ of the integral characteristic polynomial of $A$, hence is monic integral by Gauss's lemma. Its degree is $r$, so the first $r$ rows generate all later rows integrally. They are therefore a basis of $L$ itself. Both $L$ and $S$ are invariant under $A$. These facts also apply to a symmetric signed or weighted integer matrix.

### 1.1 Constructive saturation without source Smith reduction

Starting with an independent row basis $L_0$, set $S_0=L_0$. For every prime $p$ dividing $\det H$, repeat the following until the $r$ rows of $S_0$ are independent modulo $p$. Find a nonzero field relation $z^\top S_0=0\bmod p$ and normalize one coordinate $z_i=1\bmod p$. Lift all coefficients to integers with the $i$th coefficient exactly one, and replace row $i$ by $z^\top S_0/p$. This adds an integral vector to the lattice and increases it by index exactly $p$: the old basis is recovered by multiplying the new row by $p$ and subtracting the remaining combination. A nontrivial index remains at $p$ exactly when the rows are dependent modulo $p$. Each step reduces the Gram determinant by $p^2$. Every possible saturation prime divides the initial Gram determinant by the determinant identity. Therefore this finite procedure reaches $S$, and the product of the step primes is exactly $[S:L]$. For a general rectangular input, a row basis can first be found by Hermite form. Rooted Krylov input needs no such form. This algorithm uses prime factorization of the Gram determinant and finite-field elimination; it is not claimed to improve overall complexity.

### 1.2 General local metric defect formula

Fix $p$. Apply the v2.9 modular lifting algorithm to the nonsingular coordinate matrix $P$ of size $r$, not to the singular original $W$. It yields primitive adapted columns $Z$ and unit-determinant local factor $U$ with $P=UDZ^{-1}$, where $D=\operatorname{diag}(p^{a_i})$ and $a_i$ are the nonzero-source-factor valuations (including zero depths). Define $C=Z^{-1}GZ^{-\top}$ and $K=Z^\top G^{-1}Z=C^{-1}$. The Gram matrix is locally congruent to $DCD$, and $K$ is generally rational rather than integral. All Gram exponents are recovered, for example, by the exact weighted determinantal divisors

$$d_k=\min_{|I|=|J|=k}\left(\sum_{i\in I}a_i+\sum_{j\in J}a_j+v_p(\det C_{I,J})\right),\qquad b_k=d_k-d_{k-1},$$

with $d_0=0$ and zero minors of infinite valuation. This follows directly by expanding the diagonal row and column scales in each minor. In particular $\sum b_i=2\sum a_i+v_p(\det G)$. Since $(DCD)^{-1}=D^{-1}KD^{-1}$, the largest exponent has the entrywise formula

$$\boxed{b_{\max}=\max\bigl(0,\max_{i,j}(a_i+a_j-v_p(K_{ij}))\bigr).}$$

Here even depth-zero indices must be retained, because $G^{-1}$ can have nonintegral entries. This is the sharp uniform precision overhead for coefficient recovery in the independent Gram basis: recovery modulo $p^H$ needs image data modulo $p^{H+b_{\max}}$ by diagonal Smith congruences. The v2.7–2.8 smaller defect presentation applies directly only when the saturation metric is unimodular. The new metric formula is valid without that assumption. It does not make the weighted-minor method computationally cheap.

### 1.3 Four-cycle example

For $C_4$ rooted at vertex zero with cyclic labels, the first three walk rows are $e_0$, $e_1+e_3$, $2e_0+2e_2$; the fourth is $4(e_1+e_3)$. Take the saturated basis $e_0,e_1+e_3,e_2$, of Gram diagonal $(1,2,1)$. The index is two. Subtracting twice the first row from the third diagonalizes the source basis Gram to $(1,2,4)$. The full Smith profiles of $W$ and $WW^\top$ are respectively $(1,1,2,0)$ and $(1,2,4,0)$. The reduced Gram determinant is eight, the saturation determinant two. The complement is generated by $e_1-e_3$ of norm two; adjoining half the sum of the latter generator and $e_1+e_3$ produces $e_1$ and the full ambient integral lattice. Thus both source and intrinsic saturation obstructions occur in a four-vertex simple graph.

## 2. The complete integral-overlattice classification

For a rooted moment sequence $m_n$, the rational cyclic space with its form can be built abstractly: $V=\mathbb Q[x]/(M)$, $\ell(x^j)=m_j$, $\langle u,v\rangle=\ell(uv)$. Its distinguished vector is $1$ and its operator is multiplication by $x$, denoted $T$. The form is positive definite because it is the restriction of the ordinary dot product to a cyclic space of a symmetric real matrix. In the integral monomial basis $L=\mathbb Z[x]/(M)$ it has the Gram matrix $(m_{i+j})_{0\le i,j<r}$. All rooted realizations of the sequence give the same marked rational quadratic space and the same integral walk lattice, via $x^j\mapsto A^j e_o$. Here $r$ is the Hankel rank. The operator $T$ is self-adjoint and preserves $L$.

Write $L^*=\{v\in V:\langle v,L\rangle\subseteq\mathbb Z\}$ and $\mathcal D=L^*/L$. This finite group has order $\det H$ and bilinear form $\bar b(x,y)=\langle x,y\rangle\bmod\mathbb Z$. In monomial coordinates it is $H^{-1}\mathbb Z^r/\mathbb Z^r$; the form is nondegenerate since $(L^*)^*=L$. In this note isotropic means that the *entire bilinear restriction* vanishes, not just that each element has zero norm at odd primes; it applies equally at two. There is no even-lattice hypothesis.

**Theorem.** Integral overlattices $S$ of $L$ in the same rational space correspond bijectively to isotropic subgroups $I$ of $\mathcal D$. The correspondence takes $S$ to $S/L$ and inverts it by taking the preimage of $I$ in $L^*$. Moreover

$$\boxed{S^*/S\cong I^\perp/I,\qquad \det G_S=|\mathcal D|/|I|^2.}$$

Proof: integrality of $S$ puts it in $L^*$, and is equivalent to the vanishing of the form on $I\times I$. The dual lattice $S^*$ is the preimage of $I^\perp$. Taking the quotient proves the assertion, and nondegeneracy gives $|I^\perp|=|\mathcal D|/|I|$. The source Smith group is $S/L=I$. Thus the scalar data do not select $I$, but give a finite list of all abstract integral candidates and their residual discriminant modules. The self-adjoint operator $T$ preserves $L^*$ because it preserves $L$; it induces an endomorphism of $\mathcal D$. The corresponding overlattice is $T$-stable if and only if $T(I)\subseteq I$. This is necessary for a saturation arising from an integral symmetric operator. Not every such abstract candidate embeds primitively in the desired standard ambient lattice, and not every integral operator is a simple graph.

### 2.1 Full-rank standard realizations and a finite graph filter

If $r=N$, the ambient saturation must be the standard integral Euclidean lattice. Thus all realizations arise by choosing a $T$-invariant subgroup $I=I^\perp$, and requiring its integral overlattice to be isometric to $\mathbb Z^r$. Conversely, any such isometry takes $T$ to an integral symmetric matrix and the unit vector $1$ to a signed standard coordinate, with exactly the original rooted moments. Standardness is an additional condition; unimodularity alone is not asserted to imply it. It can be decided by enumerating vectors of squared norm one, a finite search by positive definiteness. In any integral positive definite lattice, two norm-one vectors not equal up to sign are orthogonal: their integral inner product has absolute value at most one and equality is possible only for equal or opposite vectors. A set of $r$ distinct axes of norm one is therefore an orthonormal basis of a unimodular integral lattice. Every orthonormal integral basis differs only by a signed permutation. Because $1$ has norm one, choose it as the first axis. For simple-graph realizations additionally require the matrix of $T$ to have zero diagonal and all off-diagonal entries zero or one after a choice of the other basis signs. This gives an exhaustive finite filter up to rooted graph isomorphism, not a claim of efficient graph reconstruction.

### 2.2 Primitive embeddings and gluing for rank-deficient realizations

There is an analogous necessary and sufficient completion criterion at a specified ambient order $N\ge r$. For each integral $T$-stable candidate $S$ from Section 2, choose a positive definite integral complement lattice $J$ of rank $N-r$. Give $J$ a self-adjoint integral endomorphism $T_J$. Form the discriminant groups $D_S=S^*/S$, $D_J=J^*/J$ with their bilinear pairings. A primitive orthogonal embedding in a unimodular ambient lattice corresponds to an anti-isometry $\phi:D_S\to D_J$ (an isomorphism negating the bilinear pairing). Its graph is isotropic and self-dual in $D_S\oplus D_J$. Its preimage in $S^*\oplus J^*$ is an integral unimodular lattice $U$ in the orthogonal rational direct sum. Both summands embed primitively: for example an element of $U$ in $S\otimes\mathbb Q$ has second discriminant class zero, forcing its first class zero. The operator $T\oplus T_J$ preserves $U$ exactly when $\phi$ intertwines the two induced discriminant endomorphisms. Require in addition that $U$ be isometric to standard $\mathbb Z^N$. This produces the necessary integral symmetric ambient operator; the norm-one-frame graph test from Section 2.1 is then the extra simple-graph criterion.

Conversely, from any standard ambient realization take $J=U\cap(S\otimes\mathbb Q)^\perp$. It is primitive, positive definite and operator-invariant. The finite subgroup $U/(S\oplus J)$ is self-dual and isotropic. Its projections to each discriminant group are injective by primitivity of the other summand: a class with first coordinate zero can be represented by a vector in the orthogonal complement and so already lies in $J$. Its order is the square root of the product of the two discriminant orders, by unimodularity. The two injections therefore force equal orders and bijective projections. The subgroup is the graph of an anti-isometry, which is equivariant because the ambient operator preserves it. This proves the criterion including the zero-rank complement (the trivial group). The criterion is structural, not an implemented enumeration of all complementary lattices or operators. In the $C_4$ example the two order-two discriminant classes of the saturation and its complement have norms $1/2$ modulo integers; their paired sum has integral norm, explaining the gluing vector explicitly.

## 3. Exhaustive seven-vertex example from v2.9

Graph I has edges 01,04,05,12,15,23,25,34,46 and root 1. Graph II has edges 01,06,12,13,14,23,34,45,56 and root 3. Their common cyclic minimal polynomial is $x^7-9x^5-4x^4+18x^3+8x^2-8x-2$, with common moment prefix $1,0,3,4,17,38,122,316,936,2550,7332,20368,57864$. The common rank is seven; $\det H=16$ and $\operatorname{SNF}(H)=(1,1,1,1,1,4,4)$. In monomial coordinates indexed 0 through 6, discriminant generators are

$$u=(1/2,0,1/2,1/2,1/2,1/4,0)^\top,\quad v=(0,1/2,0,1/2,1/2,0,3/4)^\top.$$

They are the reductions modulo integers of columns 5 and 6 of $H^{-1}$. Each has order four and they generate all 16 elements. Their pairing matrix modulo integers is $\operatorname{diag}(1,-1)/4$. The operator has $Tu=2u+3v$, $Tv=u$ modulo $L$ (from the companion matrix). A class $au+bv$ is self-isotropic exactly when $a,b$ have the same parity. An isotropic subgroup of order four is either the order-two subgroup generated by $2u,2v$, or cyclic generated by a primitive class with both coordinates odd. Up to cyclic generators the two latter subgroups are $\langle u+v\rangle$ and $\langle u-v\rangle$. All three are $T$-invariant and self-dual. This proves exhaustiveness of the candidate list without a graph census.

The original Graph I ambient lattice is the preimage of $\langle u+v\rangle$, and Graph II gives $\langle 2u,2v\rangle$, as verified by mapping the monomial coordinates to the two original vertex bases (multiplication by $W_i^\top$). The third candidate $\langle u-v\rangle$ is also standard. For clarity, in Graph I's original vertex coordinates, an explicit norm-one frame for this third lattice is $e_1,e_3$ and the four columns

$$\tfrac12(e_2-e_4-e_5+e_6),\quad\tfrac12(e_2-e_4+e_5-e_6),\quad\tfrac12(e_2+e_4-e_5-e_6),\quad\tfrac12(e_2+e_4+e_5+e_6),$$

followed by $e_0$. Their monomial coordinate classes lie in the stated subgroup by multiplication by $(W_1^\top)^{-1}$. They are orthonormal, and since the lattice is unimodular they are an integral basis. Direct application of Graph I's adjacency in this frame gives diagonal $(0,0,-1,1,-1,1,0)$. No signed permutation can make this zero. Consequently the third candidate cannot yield a simple graph in any integral orthonormal basis. The checker independently enumerates all norm-one axes for all three lattices (all vectors lie in $\frac14\mathbb Z^7$ in Graph I coordinates, so enumerate integer vectors of squared norm 16) and all $2^6$ sign assignments with the root fixed. The two known graph candidates each admit exactly one assignment in the selected frame; the third admits none. Hence the common moment sequence has exactly two rooted simple-graph realizations on seven vertices, up to rooted isomorphism. Graphs of larger order are not ruled out by this full-rank classification.

The predecessor checker was rerun and passed all 2,766 counted comparisons; its log is included separately. Counts overlap and are not independent mathematical theorems.

The singular-source extraction problem is resolved through an explicit saturated-coordinate construction. Abstract overlattices and full-rank standard realizations admit a finite classification; the rank-deficient completion criterion requires additional complementary lattice and operator data. We have not implemented general complementary-lattice enumeration, proved an efficient algorithm, classified simple graphs of unbounded ambient order, or established literature-wide novelty. The proofs use classical integral lattice duality, Smith form, and gluing. Lean certification and independent peer review remain outstanding.
